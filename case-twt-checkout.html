Simplifying checkout
from 3 steps to 2

Redesigning The Whole Truth's D2C checkout experience to reduce friction, improve information hierarchy and create a faster path to purchase.

Checkout shouldn't feel like a form.

The Whole Truth's checkout experience required customers to move through multiple stages before completing their purchase.

While each step contained necessary information, the overall journey introduced additional transitions and interactions between purchase intent and order completion.

“Can we reduce the number of steps without compromising the information required to successfully place an order?”

The opportunity was hiding in the structure.

The problem wasn't necessarily the amount of information being collected. The bigger opportunity was how that information was distributed across the checkout journey.

The solution was to bring Address and Payment into one unified checkout experience.

What I learned

01

More information doesn't necessarily mean a better experience.

Sometimes the solution is organising the same information better.

02

Every extra step in checkout has a cost.

Reducing transitions can reduce cognitive load and create a faster path to purchase.

03

UX decisions need to connect to business goals.

The objective wasn't simply to make checkout look cleaner. It was to make it easier for customers to complete a purchase.

04

Consistency matters at scale.

Working across PDP, checkout, profile and reorder helped create a more coherent D2C ecosystem.
